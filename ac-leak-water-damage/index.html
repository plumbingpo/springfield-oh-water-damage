AC Leak Water Damage Help in Springfield

Your air conditioner pulls moisture out of the air all day, and that water has to go somewhere. Normally it drips off the coil into a pan and leaves through a small drain line. When that line clogs with algae and dust, the pan fills and spills over. If the air handler sits in a hallway closet or the attic, that water ends up in the nearby floor, wall or ceiling.

Slow leaks do more damage than people expect. Drywall draws the water in, the platform beneath the unit gets saturated, and moisture slides under the floor, with mold often underway before any stain appears. Tracking the full extent of the moisture, drying it and making repairs is our part. You will still need an HVAC technician to clear the drain or fix the unit, and we can work around their visit.

First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut the System DownSwitch the system off at the thermostat so it stops making condensate. Where the wiring on the air handler is getting wet, the breaker should also go off.
  2. Avoid Wet Wiring and LightsUntil that area's power has been shut off, keep your hands away from the unit and from any outlet or ceiling light with water in or near it.
  3. Clear Water From the PanIf you can reach the pan safely, soak up or wet-vac the standing water so it stops spilling over. Crawling over a wet attic to get there is not worth it.
  4. Soak Up What You CanMove clothes, boxes and furniture back from the stained area or the closet, catch ceiling drips in a bucket, wipe up hard floors and blot the carpet.
  5. Bring In Two ProsCall us to check how far the water spread, and call an HVAC technician to clear the drain line. Keep the AC off until the repair is made.

The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • Units that rely on a condensate pump, when that pump quits or gets unplugged.
  • Coil icing brought on by a filter that was not changed or a refrigerant charge that is low.
  • Sweating ducts and pipes in a humid attic wetting the drywall under them.
  • Drain pans that sit unevenly or have corroded and split.
  • Ice on the evaporator coil melting all at once and overwhelming the pan.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Mold Near the Return

Mold may appear in 24 to 48 hours on damp materials by the air handler, where the return air stream can carry its spores through the ductwork to other rooms.

Floors Outside the Closet

Water runs out under the closet door and gets beneath carpet pad, laminate or hardwood. Hardwood cups when it is not dried, while laminate swells for good.

Drywall Below the Air Handler

When the air handler is overhead, the ceiling under it takes the water. Staining is the early sign, sagging follows, and drywall left wet for days can come down into the room below.

Trim and Baseboards

Baseboards and door casings near the closet draw water up from the floor. Because pressed-wood trim puffs up and falls apart when wet, new material goes in.

Coverage

Does Insurance Cover It?

Coverage for an AC leak usually comes down to timing. Water damage is paid for under many homeowners policies when the overflow was sudden and you responded as soon as you discovered it. If the adjuster decides the drip went on for weeks or months, it may be treated as a gradual leak or a maintenance issue and denied. Either way, the cost of fixing the AC unit is typically left out. Check your policy, and we can supply readings and photos for the claim.
