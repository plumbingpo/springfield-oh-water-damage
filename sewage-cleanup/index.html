A sewage backup is one of the most hazardous situations a property owner can face. Pathogens in raw sewage include salmonella, giardia, hepatitis and E. coli, along with other viruses, parasites and bacteria, and contact with anything contaminated is a serious risk to health. Springfield Restoration Pros provides emergency sewage cleanup throughout Springfield, responding 24/7 with properly trained technicians, appropriate personal protective equipment and EPA-registered disinfectants to decontaminate your property safely.

Water damage comes in categories, and sewage sits in the worst one: Category 3, called black water, the highest level of contamination and danger. This classification requires the most stringent cleanup protocols, including full personal protective equipment, containment barriers, removal of all porous materials that contacted sewage and thorough disinfection of every remaining surface. Our technicians follow IICRC S500 standards for Category 3 water damage so your property is restored to safe occupancy standards.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

Warning Signs for Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Dark residue from sewage left on the basement floor or ringing a drain
  • Family members falling ill, especially with gastrointestinal trouble, for no obvious reason
  • Toilets or drains that gurgle whenever you run water somewhere else
  • Dark, dirty water or sewage rising out of a tub, toilet or floor drain
  • A cleanout that weeps sewage, or wet foul patches in the lawn along the sewer path
  • More than one drain in the house overflowing or backing up together
  • Wet spots on floors or walls accompanied by foul odors

Sewage Cleanup: What Homeowners Ask

Standard homeowner's insurance often does NOT cover sewage backup unless you have a specific sewage or water backup endorsement. We strongly recommend adding this coverage, which is typically inexpensive ($50 to $100 per year) but provides critical protection. Look at your policy documents, or call your agent to confirm.
Sewage cleanup costs in Springfield typically range from $2,000 to $10,000 or more, depending on the extent of contamination and the area affected. Small contained backups may cost $1,500 to $3,000, while extensive sewage flooding can exceed $15,000 including material replacement and reconstruction.
A blocked sewer line on the property is the most common starting point. Roots from nearby trees, flushed wipes and accumulated grease account for a large share of those clogs. Other possibilities are a failed old pipe and a municipal sewer overloaded by heavy rain, which sends sewage up through floor drains. A camera run through the line after cleanup identifies the actual cause.
Emergency sewage removal and decontamination typically takes 1 to 3 days to complete. Complete restoration, including material replacement, may take 1 to 3 weeks depending on the extent of damage. Throughout, the focus is on returning your home to a safe, livable state without delay.
Some things can be kept and others cannot. We can generally clean and disinfect non-porous belongings, for example glass, metal, hard plastic and tile that is sealed. Porous materials that contacted sewage, including carpet, padding, drywall, insulation, mattresses and upholstered furniture, have to be discarded. Each discarded item is documented, giving you a record to use with your insurer.

Why Professional Sewage Cleanup Matters

Sewage cleanup is not something any homeowner should attempt. The biological hazards in raw sewage require specialized training, proper personal protective equipment (Tyvek suits, respirators, protective gloves and boots) and EPA-registered disinfection products that are not available to consumers. People exposed to the pathogens in sewage can become seriously ill with skin and respiratory infections, hepatitis or gastrointestinal disease.

Professional sewage cleanup also requires proper disposal of contaminated materials as biohazard waste. It is illegal to throw sewage-contaminated insulation, drywall or carpet in the regular trash, and doing so puts the community and the workers who collect it at risk. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Every 2 to 3 years, get a sewer camera run through the line to spot developing issues
  • Wipes clog sewer lines even when the label says flushable, so only toilet paper goes down the toilet
  • Keep downspouts and sump pump discharge out of the sanitary sewer so it does not overload in heavy rain
  • Fit a strainer over each sink and shower drain so hair and debris stay out of the pipes
  • A backwater valve at the sewer connection stops the city system from backing up into your home
  • Never pour grease, oil or food waste down your drains, since these cause blockages
