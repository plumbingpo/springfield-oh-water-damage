Structural drying is the most important phase of water damage restoration, and the one untrained responders most often get wrong. Once standing water has been extracted, a great deal of moisture is still held inside the structure: in insulation, concrete, subfloors, wood framing, drywall and other components. This trapped moisture must be scientifically removed through controlled evaporation and dehumidification to prevent the mold growth, wood rot, material degradation and odor problems that develop when structures remain wet.

Psychrometry is the science of how air temperature, humidity and moisture interact, and the IICRC-certified technicians who dry structures for Springfield Restoration Pros know it well. Using this knowledge, they calculate the specific equipment placement, temperature settings and airflow patterns needed to create the right evaporation conditions for each drying situation. Plaster, concrete, hardwood and drywall each dry at their own rate, so each needs its own approach to get down to an acceptable moisture level with no added damage.

Every day of the drying process, we check moisture using thermal imaging cameras, thermo-hygrometers and calibrated moisture meters. We document moisture readings throughout the drying process, creating a detailed drying log that shows progressive moisture reduction in all affected materials. Each day we use those numbers to reposition or retune the equipment so drying stays efficient. We call a job dry when every affected material meets the moisture standard in the IICRC S500 guidelines, and never just because a surface feels dry.

Signs You Need Structural Drying in Springfield

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Hardwood floors that are cupping, crowning or showing gaps between boards
  • A stale, musty smell that lingers even though the water was extracted
  • After water damage, a meter shows elevated moisture in the floor, wall or ceiling
  • Swollen trim and baseboards separating at the seams
  • Humidity that will not come down in the affected space, whatever ventilation you try
  • Materials that feel damp or cool to the touch even after visible water is gone
  • Drywall that is soft, swollen or shows moisture staining

The Benefits of Professional Structural Drying

There is real science behind structural drying, which is why guessing does not work. Simply placing fans and hoping for the best is the most common cause of secondary mold damage after water events. It involves daily monitoring, a calculation of the required grain depression (the capacity to remove moisture) and a working knowledge of the way materials absorb moisture and let it go. Without this knowledge, under-drying leaves moisture that feeds mold, while over-drying can damage wood and other materials.

The equipment used for professional structural drying is fundamentally different from consumer products. High-velocity air movers produce precise airflow patterns that speed up evaporation from the structure, and low-grain refrigerant (LGR) commercial dehumidifiers capture 10 to 20 times the moisture a home unit can. Between the investment in equipment and the training needed to run it, it is no surprise that DIY drying falls well short of a professional job.

How to Prevent Structural Drying Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Until a technician removes the equipment, keep it running day and night without breaks
  • Moisture can sit deep in a material whose surface feels dry, so rely on the meter readings
  • Each unit is positioned according to the moisture map, so ask before shifting anything
  • Keep windows and doors closed in areas being dried so the dehumidifiers stay efficient
  • Do not place personal items on top of drying equipment or block the airflow from air movers
  • Address any water intrusion immediately, because the faster drying begins, the better the outcome
