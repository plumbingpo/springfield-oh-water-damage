Dealing With Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in Springfield

Sump pumps do a single task, yet they commonly quit right when that task matters. Groundwater rises in a big storm and pours into the pit, right as the electricity fails, the float jams or the inflow outruns the pump. Water rises over the rim of the pit and spreads across the basement floor. Nothing stops it until some pump is working again.

Clean is the wrong word for what comes out of a sump pit. Having passed through the ground, it usually holds bacteria, silt and drain tile residue, which makes it Category 2 at best, or Category 3 once it has been standing or sewage has mixed in. That changes the cleanup: porous materials like carpet pad and wet drywall usually come out, and everything left is cleaned and disinfected before drying starts.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Keep Out of the BasementDo not step into a flooded basement while the power is on. Outlets, the pump cord or the furnace might already be wet and live.
  2. Turn Off the Basement CircuitsBasement breakers should be flipped off, provided you can get to the panel without standing in water. A panel surrounded by water is a job for the power company.
  3. Look at the Pump From a DistanceLook to see whether the pump came unplugged, its outlet tripped or the float is pinned against the pit wall. Do not reach into the water.
  4. Treat the Water as ContaminatedChildren and pets stay on the upper floor, anyone who has to enter wears rubber boots and gloves, and hands get washed after contact with anything wet.
  5. Save What Is Dry, Then CallGet dry valuables to a higher floor, snap photos showing the pump and how high the water is, and give us a call. Do not wait for the water to drain away by itself.
Coverage

What About Insurance?

This is one of the most misunderstood claims. Water backing up or overflowing from a sump is usually excluded under a standard homeowners policy, and a flood policy is a different product with rules of its own. Unless you bought an endorsement for sump overflow or water backup there is often no coverage, and such endorsements are capped. Your declarations page will list it, or your agent can tell you. Our records of the failed pump, how high the water rose and what it damaged go into your claim file.

Common Causes

How It Usually Starts

  • A discharge line that is blocked, crushed or disconnected outside the house.
  • A float switch that is stuck, tangled or pinned against the basin wall.
  • Dirt and small rocks washed into the basin that lock up the pump.
  • An old motor that burned out after years of cycling on and off.
  • A tripped outlet or breaker, or a plug knocked loose, leaving the pump without power.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Finished Basement Walls

Drywall and insulation wick dirty water upward from the slab, well above the visible water line. Because the water is contaminated, the wet lower section is cut out, not dried.

Carpet and Flooring

Groundwater-soaked carpet and padding come out, as cleaning cannot make them safe. Laminate and vinyl plank trap silty water underneath and usually come up too.

Bottom Plates and Studs

Framing lumber at floor level takes on groundwater before anything else in the wall. With the wall opened up, this wood is scrubbed, treated and then dried to normal readings.

Stored Belongings

Contaminated water is absorbed fast by fabrics, books, cardboard and the legs of furniture. Within days mold shows up on absorbent things that were in it, and they are difficult to disinfect.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Get the Water Out

Because the pumps and extraction units are ours, the broken sump does not hold up the work. We clear silt and standing water off the floor, out of low areas and from around the pit.

2

Remove Contaminated Materials

Our team wears protective gear to remove wet drywall, insulation, carpet and pad reached by groundwater, then bags and hauls it off, leaving bare framing and concrete.

3

Clean and Sanitize

An antimicrobial product goes on after the concrete, the studs and any kept belongings have been washed. Doing it ahead of drying keeps the air movers from spreading contaminants through the basement.

4

Dry and Confirm

Typically for 3 to 5 days, air movers and dehumidifiers stay in place, until the concrete and framing read normal on a moisture meter. Before we pull our equipment, the pit should have a pump that works.
